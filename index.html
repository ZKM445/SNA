Yukon First Nations Education Ecosystem Map

Background: A Yukon First Nations education ecosystem map was created after a series of gatherings (2024-25) between the different Yukon education entities in order to better understand the education ecosystem as a whole - identifying the educational players and their connections - for the benefit of Indigenous youths’ learning journeys. This has now been turned into a social network analysis visual to better identify who is in the education ecosystem, the themes of their work, the collaborations and the closeness of those collaborations, and in which communities services/programming are being provided.

The content is intended to echo what I have heard in conversations with partners in the ecosystem and through survey work. But if anything has been misrepresented, please feel free to contact me: education@yfned.ca.

Adjacent supports outside education
This page identifies and provides information on organizations and government teams that live primarily outside of education-focused work for anyone working to build up and support Indigenous youth on their education pathways. It creates a more comprehensive picture of who is out there and perhaps could offer opportunities for new forms of collaboration and funding to exist for the benefit of Indigenous youth living in the Yukon. We recognize that this is only a snapshot in time of who exists and what work they do. It is a starting point for conversation and for building new collaborative opportunities. It should also be noted that these are organizations that are listed as members of the Territorial Youth Collective, a body of organizations who meet and collaborate to respond to the priorities of the Territorial Youth Strategy.

Territorial Youth Collective

Territorial Youth Strategy

The Territorial Youth Strategy is divided into six calls to action based on youth input. These, in turn, are broken down into more specific needs and actions.

Mission: All Yukon youth have a sense of belonging throughout the Territory. This means that they feel empowered, have a positive sense of self and their futures, have mutually caring and fulfilling relationships, and feel connected to and supported by their family, community, and land.

Foundational values

  • Meaningful Consideration of the Rights of Children and Youth
  • Intersectionality
  • Yukon First Nations Ways of Knowing, Doing, & Being
  • Youth voice, Leadership, Participation

Six calls to action

Basic Needs
Community Safety & Harm Reduction
Mental Wellness & Connection
Access to Learning & Education
Employment & Career Opportunities
Land & Stewardship

First Nation School Board

Youth Leadership Table

Who: FNSB school youth – 10 out of 11 FNSB schools (26 learners) When: May 5–9, 2026

Why: To share personal concerns with the FNSB Board of Trustees around their schooling, identify needs, connect needs to broader systemic themes, and create action plans to address those needs.

Youth recommendations

  • FNSB schools sports league (coaches, equipment, transportation)
  • More diverse course selection (exposure to different career options)
  • Keep good teachers and weed out bad ones (educator training on being trauma informed, mental first aid, social-emotional trainings, learning about communities and their histories)
  • Hunting class for high schools to run every year
  • More on-the-land learning opportunities (Learning traditional skills, trips)
  • Better food program for all schools with learners as active participants in the cooking (fundamentals of cooking opportunities)
  • On the land cultural camps following the seasonal rounds (gaining wilderness first aid, knowledge keepers)

Plus: Grey Mountain Primary School (separate engagement, May 21, 2026)

Youth Recommendations (K-3):

  • Making math easier
  • Video game day
  • Trampoline day
  • Writing support
  • Carving
  • Canoeing
  • More tech time
  • Nature walks
  • Gardening
  • Breakdancing
  • Camping
